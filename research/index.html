research

Current doctoral interests and source-verified completed research by Tongrui Zhang.

Research map

My doctoral research explores the perception-to-navigation pipeline for embodied intelligent systems: how a robot observes its surroundings, forms a stable spatial representation, localizes itself, associates observations with maps, and uses that understanding to move reliably.

Current research interests

Perception for embodied systems

Learning visual and multimodal representations that remain informative under dynamic scenes, incomplete observations, and resource-constrained deployment.

Localization and spatial memory

Combining geometry, temporal evidence, and learned features to recognize places and maintain consistent spatial context.

Uncertainty-aware sensor fusion

Integrating heterogeneous observations while accounting for ambiguity, changing reliability, and disagreement between sensing channels.

Visual navigation and map intelligence

Connecting perception, localization, map matching, and route-level reasoning for robust mobile-robot navigation.

The long-term goal is to develop spatial-intelligence methods that are robust enough for real environments, transparent enough to diagnose, and efficient enough to deploy. These are current and prospective doctoral directions pursued under the supervision of Professor Jianda Han; they are not presented as completed publications or validated doctoral results.

Completed research record

High-dimensional radar perception

The 2023 Sensors paper introduced 3D Collapse U-Net for semantic segmentation of range–azimuth–velocity radar heatmaps. A Vision Transformer collapses the velocity dimension between the 3D encoder and 2D decoder. On the SCORP dataset, the reported mean intersection over union was 98.51 ± 0.81, compared with 97.25 ± 1.78 for convolutional collapse and 96.86 ± 0.21 for a 2D U-Net. Table 1 reports parameter sizes of 169.58 MB and 210.57 MB for the Transformer and convolutional-collapse variants, respectively, corresponding to a 19.5% reduction.

The paper’s author-contribution statement assigns methodology, software, investigation, data curation, original drafting, and visualization to Tongrui Zhang; conceptualization was shared with Yunsheng Fan.

Battery state estimation, fault diagnosis, and remaining-life prediction

Four journal studies examine complementary problems in lithium-ion battery health management:

  • A temperature-aware state-of-charge estimator that identifies Thevenin-model parameters with forgetting-factor recursive least squares and applies a truncated-singular-value-decomposition cubature Kalman filter. The publisher record reports validation at 5 °C, 25 °C, and 40 °C.
  • An online least-squares support vector machine for progressive fault diagnosis, incorporating confidence intervals to reduce false alarms and handle small, continuously arriving nonlinear data.
  • A chaotic time-series formulation for remaining useful life prediction, using mutual information, Cao’s method, maximum Lyapunov exponents, phase-space reconstruction, and an SVM. The cross-battery B0018 experiment reports R² = 0.89 and RMSE = 0.58%.
  • An improved Dempster–Shafer framework that fuses WPD-CNN and extended Kalman filter evidence. Across NASA batteries B0005, B0006, and B0007, the fused model reports R² = 0.94–0.998 and RMSE = 0.007–0.015.

The published contribution statements identify Tongrui Zhang as method/software lead and original-draft author on the 2025 studies, with Hao Sun providing supervision and shared validation.

Robotics and edge AI

My 2024 undergraduate thesis developed a remote-control and target-recognition workflow for a dual-screw-driven amphibious robot. It combined a Raspberry Pi and control electronics with an improved YOLOv8s detector, model pruning, and deployment on a Huawei Atlas 200I DK A2 edge platform. The thesis reports that the improved detector raised precision by 6.9 percentage points, mAP@0.5 by 1.1 points, and mAP@0.5:0.95 by 1.5 points against its YOLOv8s baseline while maintaining recall. Pruning reduced parameters from 11,126,358 to 8,936,846 and operations from 28.4G to 22.2G.
