<p class="academic-lede">
I am a PhD student in the College of Artificial Intelligence at Nankai University, advised by Professor Jianda Han. I study how autonomous systems can turn incomplete and changing sensory observations into reliable spatial understanding for real-world navigation.
</p>

My doctoral interests connect learning-based visual perception, localization, spatial representation, multi-sensor information fusion, and map-aware navigation for mobile robots. My earlier research spans radar semantic segmentation, lithium-ion battery diagnosis and remaining-life prediction, and visual perception for an amphibious robot. Across these projects, I have worked with high-dimensional sensing, machine learning, state estimation, evidence fusion, and edge deployment.

## Current research interests

<div class="academic-grid">
  <div class="academic-card">
    <h3>Perception for embodied systems</h3>
    <p>Learning scene representations that remain useful under viewpoint changes, environmental complexity, incomplete observations, and deployment constraints.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="academic-card">
    <h3>Localization and spatial memory</h3>
    <p>Combining geometric and learned cues to estimate position, represent places, and maintain consistent spatial context over time.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="academic-card">
    <h3>Uncertainty-aware sensor fusion</h3>
    <p>Integrating complementary observations while reasoning about reliability, ambiguity, and changing sensor quality.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="academic-card">
    <h3>Visual navigation and map intelligence</h3>
    <p>Closing the loop from perception to localization and map matching so mobile robots can navigate with greater robustness and interpretability.</p>
  </div>
</div>

My M.Eng. in Mechanical Engineering (Energy and Environment) was formally awarded by the University of Calgary in June 2026.
